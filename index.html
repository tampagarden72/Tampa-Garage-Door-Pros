Why Garage Door Springs Break in Tampa and What to Do Next

Heat, humidity and salt air are hard on the steel that lifts your door. Here is how to spot a failing spring, how long one should last and how to handle it safely.

Written for Tampa homeowners • 6 minute read • Updated November 2026

On this page Why springs failWarning signsHow long they lastSafe next stepsStorm seasonQuestions

Tampa Garage Door Pros gets more calls about broken springs than almost any other problem in the area. Florida heat and humidity make steel coils tire much earlier than most owners expect, and a small crack can turn into a stuck door overnight. This guide explains the warning signs, and if you already see them, our spring replacement specialists can help.

Quick answers

  • Most torsion springs are built for a set number of open and close cycles, and Tampa weather can shorten that life.
  • A loud bang from the garage and a door that will not lift usually mean a spring has snapped.
  • Do not use the opener on a door with a broken spring, and do not try to wind springs yourself.
  • Springs should be replaced in pairs and matched to the exact weight of your door.

Why springs fail earlier in Florida

A garage door is heavy, often between 150 and 400 pounds. The springs above the opening carry nearly all of that weight, so the opener only has to guide the door along its path. Every time the door moves, the spring winds and unwinds, and the metal slowly tires.

Tampa adds three extra problems. Humid air sits on bare steel and encourages rust. Salt in the air near the bay and the waterfront neighborhoods speeds up corrosion. Long summers keep the garage hot, which dries out lubricant faster than most owners expect. A rusty coil has tiny pits on its surface, and a crack usually starts at one of them.

Daily use matters too. A family that opens the door four times a day uses up spring cycles much faster than a household that parks outside and only opens it on weekends.

Warning signs to watch for

Springs rarely fail without hints. Listen and look whenever the door runs, and compare what you notice with the table below.

Safety note: a torsion spring stores a large amount of energy. A slipped winding bar can cause serious injury, which is why this repair belongs to trained technicians with the right tools.

How long a garage door spring should last

Standard springs are commonly rated for about 10,000 cycles. One cycle is a single open and a single close. At four cycles a day, that is roughly seven years. Higher cycle springs, rated at 20,000 or more, can double that figure and are often worth the small extra cost for busy households.

Wire size and length also affect lifespan. A spring that is too light for the door works harder than it should and wears out sooner. This is why a replacement needs to be matched to the door instead of copied from a similar one.

Replace springs in pairs

Many doors use two springs. When one breaks, the other has done the same amount of work and is close behind. Changing both at once keeps the door balanced and saves a second visit. What to do when a spring breaks

  1. Leave the door where it isDo not try to lift it by hand if it feels heavy. An unbalanced door can fall.
  2. Unplug the openerThe motor is not built to lift the full weight of the door and its gears can strip.
  3. Check the cablesLook for cables hanging loose or off the drums, and keep your hands and head away from them.
  4. Use the emergency release only if the door is fully closedA door left partly open should not be disconnected.
  5. Call a professionalDescribe what you heard and saw so the technician can bring the correct spring size.

Safe to do

  • Take photos of the spring and door label
  • Keep children and pets out of the garage
  • Note the door size and weight if known

Never do

  • Loosen set screws on a wound spring
  • Use pliers or screwdrivers as winding tools
  • Run the opener to test the door

If the cables are also damaged, the repair can include new cables and drum timing. The details are covered in our guide to garage door cable repair.

Springs, storms and wind ratings

Hurricane season puts extra stress on the whole door, not just the springs. A door that is out of balance flexes more under wind pressure, and a weak door can fail at a time when your home needs it most. Hillsborough County follows the Florida Building Code, which sets wind and pressure requirements for replacement doors depending on location.

If your door is old, rusted in several sections or missing wind reinforcement, a spring replacement may only be a short term fix. Simple habits that extend spring life

You cannot stop wear, but you can slow it down.

Lubricate twice a year

Use a silicone based garage door lubricant on the coils, rollers and hinges. Avoid thick grease, which collects dust and sand.

Test the balance

With the opener disconnected and the door closed, lift it by hand to waist height. A balanced door stays in place. If it drops or pulls upward, the springs need attention.

Book a yearly inspection

A trained technician checks spring tension, cables, rollers, tracks and safety sensors, and catches small problems early. Learn what is included in a garage door maintenance and safety tune-up.

Common questions

Can I open my garage door with a broken spring?

It is possible to lift the door by hand in some cases, but it is unsafe. Without the spring, the door acts like dead weight and can drop quickly. Wait for a technician.

Do both springs need replacing if only one breaks?

Replacing both is the standard recommendation. They are the same age and have the same number of cycles behind them, so the second one tends to follow soon after.

Why did my spring break when I barely use the door?

Age and corrosion count as much as use. In Tampa, humidity and salt air can weaken a spring that has few cycles on it.

How long does a spring replacement take?

Most visits take between one and two hours, depending on the door size, cable condition and whether other parts need work.

Is a broken spring an emergency?

If a car is stuck inside or the door is open and cannot be secured, treat it as urgent. Otherwise, avoid using the door and book the first available visit.

Garage Door Opener Not Working? Common Causes and Fixes

An opener that stops responding can lock your car in or leave your home open. Start with these checks, then learn which problems need a professional.

Written for Tampa homeowners • 6 minute read • Updated November 2026

On this pageQuick checks before you callRule out the spring firstWhen repair makes senseWhen a new opener is the better callQuestions

Tampa Garage Door Pros hears from homeowners every week about an opener that suddenly stops responding. The cause is often simpler than people expect, and many fixes need nothing more than a fresh battery or a clean sensor lens. This guide covers the safe checks first, then explains when to bring in opener repair and troubleshooting.

Quick answers

  • Dead remotes, blocked sensors and tripped power are the easiest causes to check first.
  • A motor that hums but the door stays put often points to a stripped gear or a broken spring.
  • Blinking sensor lights mean the safety beam is blocked or misaligned.
  • Opener problems and spring problems look alike, so rule out the spring before replacing the motor.

Quick checks before you call

Most opener complaints have a simple cause. Work through these in order and stop if anything feels unsafe.

Confirm the opener is plugged in and the outlet works. Replace the remote battery and try the wall button. If the wall button works but the remote does not, the remote needs reprogramming or a new battery. Wipe both photo eye lenses with a dry cloth, because dust and sunlight can confuse them.

Rule out the spring first

The opener does not lift the door by force. The springs do that work. When a spring breaks, the motor strains against the full weight and can strip its gears in a single use. Replacing the motor without fixing the spring only repeats the failure. If you heard a loud bang before the problem began, read our guide on broken garage door spring replacement before touching the opener.

When repair makes sense

A technician can often fix gears, circuit boards, travel limits and sensor wiring on the same visit. Our garage door opener repair service covers chain, belt and screw drive models, along with remote and keypad programming.

Repair is usually the better choice when the unit is under ten years old and the damage is limited to one part.

When a new opener is the better call

Older openers can lack modern safety features such as dependable auto reverse and rolling code security. Humid Florida air also corrodes circuit boards over time. If repairs keep returning, consider smart opener installation with a quiet belt drive, Wi-Fi control and battery backup for storm outages.

Common questions

Why does my opener hum but not lift the door?

The drive gear may be stripped, or a spring may have broken so the motor cannot lift the weight. Do not keep pressing the button.

Can I use the door manually?

Only if the door moves easily and the springs are intact. A heavy door can fall, so stop if it feels unbalanced.

How long do openers last?

Often 10 to 15 years, depending on use, power quality and humidity.

Why does the door reverse on its own?

Usually the safety sensors are blocked or out of line, or the close force setting needs adjusting.

Hurricane Rated Garage Doors in Tampa: What Homeowners Should Know

In a major storm, the garage door is often the largest opening on your home. Here is what wind ratings mean and how to choose a door that suits Tampa Bay.

Written for Tampa homeowners • 7 minute read • Updated November 2026

On this pageWhy the garage door is the weak pointWhat a wind rating tells youWhat to look for in a replacementBefore the storm season startsQuestions

Storm season makes the garage door a serious part of your home's protection, and Tampa Garage Door Pros explains what the ratings mean in plain terms. Wind labels can look confusing, yet they say a lot about how a door behaves under pressure. Read on to see what to check, including how impact garage door installation fits into a storm ready home.

Quick answers

  • A garage door that fails in high wind can let pressure build inside the house.
  • Wind rated doors are tested for pressure and, in many cases, flying debris.
  • Reinforced struts, strong track brackets and the right opener all matter.
  • Ask your insurer which documents are needed for wind mitigation credit.

Why the garage door is the weak point

Most garage doors are large, flat panels held by tracks and rollers. Under strong gusts, panels can bow and tracks can pull from the wall. Once the door opens, wind enters the garage and pushes up on the roof from below.

This is why building rules in Florida treat garage doors as part of the home's structure and not a simple convenience.

What a wind rating tells you

A rating label lists the design pressure the door was tested to resist, in pounds per square foot, and the wind speeds it is approved for. Doors approved for impact are also tested against flying debris. Your location, home height and exposure to open water all affect what rating is required, and your installer and local building department confirm the right one.

What to look for in a replacement

Choose a door with a posted product approval and ask who handles permits and inspections. Compare insulation, window options and finish as well, since salt air attacks cheap hardware. Our team covers these choices on the wind-load rated garage door installation page, and you can browse styles for homes at residential garage door options in Tampa.

Before the storm season starts

Whatever door you own, keep it in good shape. Check that springs are balanced, rollers turn freely and bolts are tight. A yearly garage door safety inspection catches loose brackets and worn parts before a storm tests them. Never leave the opener running during a power surge, and know how to release the door manually when the power is out.

Common questions

Do all Tampa garage doors need a hurricane rating?

Replacement doors generally have to meet local wind requirements. Your installer can confirm what applies at your address.

Will a new door lower my insurance bill?

Some insurers offer wind mitigation credits for approved doors. Ask yours what proof they accept.

Can I reinforce my existing door?

Bracing kits exist for some doors, but they do not replace a tested, approved door.

Should I replace the opener too?

Not always. The opener should match the door weight, and battery backup helps during outages.
